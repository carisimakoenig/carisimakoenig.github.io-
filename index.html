Finding Caravaggio

A Search Across American Collections

Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio (1571–1610), known simply as Caravaggio, was an Italian Baroque painter whose dramatic use of light and shadow, uncompromising realism, and unconventional depictions of religious and everyday subjects transformed European painting. His work challenged the artistic conventions of his time, presenting sacred figures as ordinary people with an extraordinary sense of humanity and immediacy. More than four centuries later, his paintings remain as compelling and provocative as when they were first created.

Most of Caravaggio's surviving paintings remain in Europe, particularly Italy, but a small number have found their way into American museum collections. Where are they, and how many can one actually see?

This website began with that question and a growing curiosity about the paintings scattered across the United States. It is a simple inventory of Caravaggio's works in American collections, noting which I have seen and which remain on my list. Along the way, it offers an opportunity to learn more about the paintings, their histories, and the institutions that care for them.

The ambition is to see them all. The fun is in finding them. Here’s where the search begins.
