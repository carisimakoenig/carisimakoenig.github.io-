Completed 1604. Acquired 1952

Caravaggio portrays a youthful John the Baptist seated in a dark landscape, his expression contemplative and withdrawn. Stripped of traditional religious symbolism, the figure becomes a study of human vulnerability and introspection. Dramatic contrasts of light and shadow intensify the painting’s emotional depth. The Nelson-Atkins Museum acquired it in 1952. The painting was orginally commissioned for Ottavio Costa's private chapel on his estate near Genoa, Italy.
